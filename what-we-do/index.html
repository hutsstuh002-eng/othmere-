WHAT WE DO

Businesses do not always have the capability they require.

Othmere provides a way for businesses to obtain external capabilities when a particular requirement cannot, or does not need to, be addressed internally.

THE REQUIREMENT

Knowing what is required does not always mean knowing where to obtain it.

Organizations regularly encounter requirements that sit outside their existing capabilities. A business may need a specialized technical function, additional operational capacity, professional expertise, or a service that is not practical to maintain internally.

In many cases, the difficulty is not identifying the requirement itself. The difficulty is determining what capability is appropriate, where that capability can be obtained, and whether the organization providing it is suitable for the particular circumstances.

Othmere exists to address that part of the problem.

THE ROLE

Othmere begins with the requirement, not with a predetermined service.

The nature of a requirement determines what capability is relevant. Othmere therefore does not operate around a fixed assumption that every business needs the same type of service or the same type of organization.

01

Understand

Establish what the business actually requires, including the intended outcome, scope, constraints and circumstances surrounding the requirement.

02

Determine

Establish the type of external capability appropriate to the requirement rather than beginning with a predetermined provider or service.

03

Assess

Consider whether the available capability and organization are suitable for the particular requirement and its relevant conditions.

04

Arrange

Establish an appropriate route for the required capability to be provided and for the resulting engagement to proceed.

SCOPE

The requirement defines the scope.

Othmere is not limited to a predetermined list of services. The capabilities that can be addressed will change as requirements change, and a requirement that does not correspond to an existing category can still be considered on its own terms.

Areas such as technology, cybersecurity, automation, professional services and business operations represent examples of capabilities that may be relevant. They are not intended to define the boundaries of Othmere's role.

EXTERNAL CAPABILITY

External does not mean arbitrary.

Obtaining a capability outside an organization can be practical when building that capability internally would require unnecessary time, cost, infrastructure or specialization.

01

Specialization

Certain requirements depend on expertise that is better maintained by organizations focused on that particular capability.

02

Capacity

An organization may possess the relevant knowledge while lacking the internal capacity required at a particular time.

03

Independence

Some requirements are better addressed by an organization that operates independently of the business requesting the capability.

04

Practicality

Maintaining a capability internally is not always the most practical way to obtain the result that the business requires.

THE RELATIONSHIP

Othmere does not replace the organizations that provide the capability.

Othmere's role is to address the requirement and establish an appropriate route to its fulfillment. The organization ultimately performing the relevant work remains responsible for the substantive service it provides.

This distinction allows the roles of the business requiring the capability, Othmere, and the organization providing the capability to remain clear throughout an engagement.
